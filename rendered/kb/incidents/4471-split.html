parser · the shared parser reads the month first

What people see. A booking lands on the wrong date, with day and month swapped: 7 April goes out as 4 July. It happens on every channel: the app, email and the form.

Why. The shared parser reads every date month first. Our tenants in day-first locales (Germany, the Netherlands, Poland, the UK and others) write the day first, so their dates come out flipped.

Where it stands. fixed· fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. Reports of a swapped date from a day-first tenant that arrive after 5 October mean the fix didn’t reach them: open a new issue rather than reopening #4471.

The rule. The tenant’s locale writes the day before the month, and the report did not come in through a booking-v3 form. This is causeOf in causes.ts, unchanged.

hint · the booking-v3 form tells people to type the month first

What people see. The same swap, day and month, but only in bookings made through the booking form, and only for tenants on booking-v3.

Why. booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People do what the hint says and type the month first. The form then reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. The parser is not involved: fix the parser and these bookings still come out wrong. The fix is the hint (or the form reading what its hint asks for).

Where it stands. won't fix· booking-v3 form retired. The form is gone, so no new reports of this kind should arrive. One that does means a tenant is still on booking-v3.

The rule. A day-first tenant on booking-v3, and the report came in through the form. This is the hint branch of causeOf.

one-day · the date moves by one day, not a swap

What people see. The date is off by exactly one day: Tuesday shows as Monday. Day and month are where they should be. Two people invited to the same meeting from different time zones can get different days (R-41344: a client in Lisbon got Tuesday, one in Toronto got Monday).

Why. This is a time zone or day boundary problem: a time is turned into a date in the wrong zone and falls on the other side of midnight. It has nothing to do with the order of day and month, so neither the parser fix nor retiring booking-v3 touches it.

Where it stands. open. This is the only problem from #4471 that is still broken, and nobody owns it.

The rule. Tenant and channel can’t show this one. The desk finds it by reading what the reporter wrote, and lists those reports in desk-notes.json (oneDayMove). A report on that list is one-day, before any other rule. The #4471 page files these under parser with a footnote; they belong apart.

Why one ticket hid three problems

The desk merges by subject line (decision 0001). The subjects here are things like “Date is wrong”, “Wrong day on the invite” and “Month and day swapped?”, and each of those turns up under all three problems. A subject says how the reporter felt, not what broke. Merging on it put all three problems in one place, and the biggest one gave the ticket its name. Decision 0002 proposes merging by cause instead; 0001 stays as it was written.

What to do now

  • one-day needs an owner. Its issue is open and unassigned; until someone takes it, the desk keeps adding reports of it there, not to #4471.
  • Don’t reopen #4471. It is closed and its two answers stand. New reports go to the issue for their problem, sorted by the rules above.
  • A swapped date after 5 October is a parser regression or a tenant still on booking-v3, not “more of #4471”. Check the tenant’s form before you file it.
  • Read the reports for one-day moves as they come in, and add them to oneDayMove in desk-notes.json. Tenant and channel will never find them for you.
